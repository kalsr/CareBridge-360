CareBridge 360, "Closing the Gaps in Modern Healthcare," was developed by Randy Singh of Kalsnet (KNet) Consulting Group. It is one web app that brings together five practical tools, each aimed at a major weakness in today's healthcare system. The first tackles clinician burnout from paperwork by drafting medical notes and insurance letters. The second tackles late detection of chronic disease problems by monitoring patients' home readings. The third improves access in underserved areas through online triage and virtual visits. The fourth addresses the shortage of mental health professionals with quick screening and stepped care. The fifth fixes scattered patient records by merging them and checking them for safety problems. The app is meant for demonstration and teaching, so it shows clearly how technology can close these gaps. It is not a medical device and is not for real clinical decisions.

How it works

Every module works the same way, with three tabs. The Learn tab explains the idea in plain language and includes a glossary, a concept diagram and the formulas behind the tool. The Try it tab lets the user run the tool on built-in demo data or upload their own file. The Download tab exports the results as a PDF report, an editable Word document, a plain text file and CSV tables.

In documentation, the app reads a visit transcript and splits it into sentences. It places each sentence into the Subjective, Objective, Assessment or Plan section of a SOAP note by counting keyword matches. It also picks out vitals, medicines and suggested diagnosis codes, and it measures how complete a prior-authorization request is before drafting the letter.

In remote monitoring, it checks daily readings three ways. First, against fixed safety limits. Second, for worrying trends, such as rapid weight gain. Third, against each patient's own normal range, using a z-score. It then combines the alerts into a risk score that ranks who the nurse should call first.

In telehealth, it checks for emergency red-flag symptoms first. Otherwise, it adds up a triage score from symptoms, severity, age, chronic illness and duration. That score sets the care level: urgent, soon, routine or self-care. The app then matches the patient to the right specialty and offers video appointment slots.

In mental health, it scores the standard PHQ-9 depression and GAD-7 anxiety questionnaires and assigns severity bands. It flags any self-harm response for same-day follow-up, then recommends a step of care, from watchful waiting up to a psychiatry referral.

In care coordination, it confirms that records from a hospital, a clinic and a pharmacy belong to the same person. It then merges them and flags dangerous drug combinations, conflicting doses and duplicate lab tests. The merged record can be exported in the FHIR standard format.

The Overview page also has an impact estimator. Users enter their own assumptions there to estimate yearly value from time saved, hospital admissions avoided and duplicate tests prevented.
