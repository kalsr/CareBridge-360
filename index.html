APPLICATION OVERVIEW

CareBridge 360, developed by Randy Singh of Kalsnet(KNet) Consulting Group, is an integrated healthcare technology platform demonstrating how Artificial Intelligence, automated analytics, predictive monitoring, and intelligent decision-support technologies can address critical gaps in modern healthcare.

The platform brings together five specialized healthcare modules designed to improve clinical documentation, remote patient monitoring, telehealth accessibility, mental health screening, and healthcare data coordination.

1. WHAT THE APPLICATION IS ABOUT

CareBridge 360, "Closing the Gaps in Modern Healthcare," is designed to demonstrate how emerging technologies can address major operational and service-delivery challenges in today's healthcare environment.

The application integrates five practical tools into one web-based platform. Each tool addresses a different healthcare challenge, from clinician administrative workloads to fragmented patient records and limited access to specialized care.

The overall objective is to demonstrate how AI-driven automation, predictive analytics, intelligent screening, and data integration can improve operational efficiency and support better-informed healthcare processes.

The platform is intended for educational and technology demonstration purposes. It is not a certified medical device and is not intended for actual clinical decision-making.

2. FIVE INTEGRATED HEALTHCARE MODULES

CareBridge 360 consists of five interconnected application modules, each targeting a specific healthcare mission gap.

01. Clinical Documentation

Automates SOAP medical notes, extracts clinical information, and assists with insurance prior-authorization letters.

02. Remote Patient Monitoring

Analyzes patient readings, identifies abnormal trends, and calculates risk scores for prioritizing follow-up.

03. Telehealth & Triage

Evaluates symptoms, identifies emergency warning signs, and supports virtual healthcare appointment matching.

04. Mental Health Screening

Evaluates PHQ-9 and GAD-7 questionnaires, identifies severity levels, and supports stepped-care recommendations.

05. Healthcare Data Coordination

Integrates fragmented patient records and identifies potential medication, dosage, and laboratory inconsistencies.

3. HOW THE APPLICATION WORKS

Every module provides three primary interactive tabs that guide users through learning, executing, and exporting the application results.

LEARN

Explains the underlying technology and operational concepts using understandable descriptions, technical glossaries, conceptual diagrams, and mathematical formulas.

TRY IT

Allows users to execute the application using built-in synthetic demonstration datasets or upload their own supported files for processing and analysis.

DOWNLOAD

Provides downloadable analytical results and reports in PDF, editable Microsoft Word, plain text, and CSV formats.

4. CLINICAL DOCUMENTATION AUTOMATION

The documentation module processes medical visit transcripts and separates the information into the four standard SOAP documentation categories: Subjective, Objective, Assessment, and Plan.

It analyzes sentences using keyword matching, extracts available vital signs and medication information, and identifies suggested diagnosis codes.

The module also evaluates the completeness of prior-authorization requests and assists with generating supporting insurance letters.

Optional Claude AI integration can generate more natural-language SOAP documentation when an API key is configured.

5. REMOTE PATIENT MONITORING & PREDICTIVE ANALYTICS

This module evaluates daily patient readings to identify potential warning conditions and prioritize individuals requiring additional attention.

It applies three analytical techniques:

  • Threshold Analysis: Compares readings against predefined safety limits.
  • Trend Analysis: Identifies potentially concerning changes over time, such as rapid weight gain.
  • Statistical Analysis: Uses Z-score calculations to compare readings against individual patient baselines.

These indicators are combined into a risk score that helps demonstrate how healthcare teams can prioritize patient follow-up.

6. TELEHEALTH & INTELLIGENT PATIENT TRIAGE

The telehealth module evaluates patient symptoms and identifies emergency warning signs before calculating a triage score.

The scoring process considers symptoms, severity, patient age, chronic conditions, and symptom duration.

Based on the resulting score, the application assigns one of four care categories: Urgent, Soon, Routine, or Self-Care.

It also demonstrates specialty matching and simulated video appointment scheduling to improve access to virtual healthcare.

7. MENTAL HEALTH SCREENING & ASSESSMENT

This module demonstrates automated analysis of two standardized screening questionnaires: PHQ-9 for depression and GAD-7 for anxiety.

The application calculates questionnaire scores, assigns severity categories, and identifies responses requiring additional human attention.

Potential self-harm responses are flagged for same-day follow-up in the demonstration workflow.

The module also presents stepped-care pathways ranging from monitoring and supportive interventions to specialist psychiatric referral.

8. HEALTHCARE DATA COORDINATION & INTEGRATION

Healthcare information is frequently distributed across hospitals, clinics, pharmacies, and other independent information systems.

This module demonstrates how information from multiple sources can be matched and consolidated into a coordinated patient record.

It identifies potential medication conflicts, inconsistent dosages, duplicate laboratory tests, and other data inconsistencies.

The consolidated information can be exported using the FHIR healthcare data-exchange standard, supporting interoperability concepts.

9. DATASETS & DEMONSTRATION ENVIRONMENT

All built-in application datasets are synthetic, meaning they are realistic demonstration records but do not represent actual patients.

The demonstration environment includes:

  • Sample doctor-patient medical visit transcripts.
  • Thirty days of simulated home readings for eight fictional patients.
  • Fifteen fictional telehealth intake forms and simulated appointment slots.
  • Repeated mental health screening records for six fictional individuals.
  • Simulated patient medication, laboratory, and allergy records distributed across three separate systems.

Users can upload supported TXT, Word, and CSV files. Each module provides templates identifying the required input columns.

Uploaded files are processed during the application session and are not stored by the application. Real patient information should only be used in an appropriately authorized and compliant environment.

These calculations help demonstrate how organizations can evaluate potential financial benefits and develop a business case for investing in healthcare technology solutions.

Its analytical outputs demonstrate automated processing and decision-support concepts. They are not substitutes for professional medical judgment.

Diagnosis, treatment, emergency response, and patient-care decisions must remain under the supervision of appropriately qualified healthcare professionals.
